Background

Ukraine's borders have been contested since 2014. On 27 March 2014 the UN General Assembly declared the 16 March Crimea referendum to have 'no validity' and reaffirmed Ukraine's territorial integrity by 100 votes to 11. Overnight on 23-24 February 2022, while the Security Council was in session, Russian President Vladimir Putin announced a 'military operation' in Ukraine. In September 2022 UN Secretary-General Antonio Guterres said any annexation of Donetsk, Luhansk, Kherson and Zaporizhzhia 'would have no legal value'. Sources: UN News (1, 2), UNRIC.

UN agencies keep the tallies. Marking the fourth anniversary in February 2026, Guterres cited more than 15,000 civilians killed and over 41,000 injured, including 3,200 children, Reuters reported. UNHCR estimated 5.9 million refugees from Ukraine recorded globally at the end of 2025, close to 5.4 million of them in Europe, and about 1.4 million who had returned for at least three months. OCHA's 2026 plan counts 10.8 million people in need inside Ukraine and seeks US$2.3 billion to assist 4.1 million. Sources: Arab News, UNHCR, UN Ukraine.

Diplomacy and bombardment run in parallel. In September 2026 Al Jazeera reported that US efforts had stalled during Washington's war with Iran and that US envoys proposed technical three-way talks. The UN human rights mission verified at least 328 civilians killed and 2,105 injured in September 2026, 85 per cent more than a year earlier, and recorded strikes on Kyiv's three main power stations on 13-14 October. On 23 October Reuters reported a local ceasefire for occupied Oleshky and a 270-for-270 prisoner exchange. Sources: Al Jazeera, UN News, Yahoo News.

Key actors

  • Ukraine (government of President Volodymyr Zelenskyy) — Defending state; Zelenskyy says any peace deal requires 'substantial security guarantees' and funding to sustain Ukraine's military for another decade. (Yahoo News)
  • Russia (government of President Vladimir Putin) — Invading state; Putin said in September 2026 that Moscow was ready to resume talks with Kyiv after Russia's parliamentary elections. (Al Jazeera)
  • United States — Mediator under President Donald Trump; envoy Steve Witkoff hosted Ukrainian, European and NATO officials in Miami in October 2026 as Trump eased Russian diesel sanctions. (Al Jazeera)
  • European Union — Backer of Kyiv; foreign policy chief Kaja Kallas announced the release of $7.5bn in military-aid-linked funds for Ukraine in September 2026. (Al Jazeera)
  • UN Human Rights Monitoring Mission in Ukraine — OHCHR mission led by Danielle Bell that verifies civilian casualties; it called early October 2026 among the deadliest periods for civilians in years. (UN News)

Timeline

  1. March 27, 2014 — The UN General Assembly declared the Crimea referendum to have 'no validity' and reaffirmed Ukraine's territorial integrity by 100 votes to 11. (UN News)
  2. February 24, 2022 — Putin announced a 'military operation' in Ukraine as the Security Council met; Guterres called it the saddest moment of his tenure and urged Russia to withdraw. (UNRIC)
  3. September 29, 2022 — Guterres said annexing Donetsk, Luhansk, Kherson and Zaporizhzhia would have 'no legal value', calling the votes held there no genuine expression of popular will. (UN News)
  4. August 2024 — Ukrainian forces pushed into Russia's Kursk region; Moscow said on 26 April 2025 it had expelled them, which Kyiv denied, AP reported. (PBS NewsHour)
  5. August 15, 2025 — Trump and Putin met in Anchorage, Alaska, without Zelenskyy; the summit ended in under three hours with no deal to end the war. (Al Jazeera)
  6. September 25, 2026 — Al Jazeera reported Washington had proposed trilateral technical talks, with the UAE suggested as host, after Russian strikes on Kyiv killed at least seven people. (Al Jazeera)
  7. October 10, 2026 — A Russian attack on Zaporizhzhia killed 20 people, including three children, a day after Trump announced a deal lifting sanctions on Russian diesel exports. (Al Jazeera)
  8. October 16, 2026 — The UN human rights mission reported at least 328 civilians killed and 2,105 injured in September, and strikes that left 600,000 Kyiv residents without electricity. (UN News)
  9. October 23, 2026 — Reuters reported a local ceasefire in occupied Oleshky from 27 October for aid and evacuations, a 270-for-270 prisoner swap and November talks. (Yahoo News)

What to watch

  • Frontline movement. The UN mission recorded civilian casualties on 22 of September 2026's 30 days; a shifting contact line changes which towns are under daily fire. Tracked in the Armed Conflict Events map layer on the live dashboard.
  • Strikes on energy infrastructure. Strikes on 13-14 October 2026 cut power to 600,000 Kyiv residents; repeats as winter deepens would be OCHA's 'crisis within a crisis'. Tracked in the Armed Conflict Events map layer on the live dashboard.
  • Refugee departures and returns. UNHCR counted 5.9 million refugees at end-2025 and 1.4 million returnees; rising departures would signal that winter attacks are displacing people again. Tracked in the UNHCR Displacement panel on the live dashboard.
  • Sanctions relief on Russian energy. Trump's October 2026 move on Russian diesel drew EU objections; further waivers or new designations show whether pressure on Moscow is easing or tightening. Tracked in the Sanctions Pressure panel on the live dashboard.
