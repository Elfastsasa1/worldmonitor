Background

Venezuela's governing movement has held power since Hugo Chávez took office in 1999, and Nicolás Maduro succeeded him in 2013. His 2018 re-election was widely seen as a sham because main opposition parties were barred. In 2024 electoral authorities declared him the winner, although the opposition gathered evidence that he lost by more than two to one, AP reported, and more than 2,000 people were arrested in the protests that followed. By September 2025 Washington had doubled its bounty for Maduro to $50 million and sent warships to the Caribbean, and on 2 September it struck a boat it said had left Venezuela carrying drugs, killing 11 people. Sources: PBS NewsHour, wlrn.org, dawn.com.

By late October 2025 the US campaign against alleged drug boats in the Caribbean and Pacific had killed at least 61 people, and UN human rights chief Volker Türk said the strikes violated international human rights law. On 3 January 2026 US forces struck targets in Caracas and seized Maduro and his wife, Cilia Flores, flying them to New York to face narco-terrorism charges. OCHA says 7.9 million people in Venezuela need humanitarian aid, and an estimated 6.87 million Venezuelans lived elsewhere in the region in 2025. Twin earthquakes on 24 June 2026 killed 6,438 people, according to an official toll in August. Sources: PBS NewsHour (1, 2), humanitarianaction.info (1, 2), english.adnkronos.com.

In early November 2026 Venezuela is run by interim President Delcy Rodríguez, Maduro's former deputy, who works closely with Washington, while Maduro awaits trial in New York and pleaded not guilty to new torture conspiracy charges on 15 October. On 22 October thousands joined the first nationwide opposition protest in nearly two years demanding elections; no date has been set. María Corina Machado returned on 25 October and called for a presidential vote in 2027. On 2 November the US reaffirmed its three-phase plan ending in democratic transition. In Apure, an army attack on an ELN camp on 30 October killed at least 19 people, AFP reported. Sources: Al Jazeera (1, 2, 3), thestar.com.my, ewn.co.za.

Key actors

  • Delcy Rodríguez — Interim president since Maduro's capture in January 2026, who has opened the state-owned oil industry to foreign companies and promised elections without setting a date. (Al Jazeera)
  • María Corina Machado — Nobel Peace Prize-winning opposition leader who returned from more than 10 months in exile in October 2026 and is pressing for a presidential election in 2027. (Al Jazeera)
  • US government (Trump administration) — Removed Maduro by force and now backs the interim government under a three-phase plan of stabilisation, economic recovery and reconciliation, then democratic transition, which Secretary of State Marco Rubio reaffirmed on 2 November 2026. (Al Jazeera)
  • Nicolás Maduro — Former president held in the United States since January 2026, who pleaded not guilty on 15 October 2026 to torture conspiracy charges added to the earlier drug trafficking case. (Al Jazeera)
  • Jorge Rodríguez — National Assembly president and brother of the interim president, removed from the US Treasury sanctions list on 14 October 2026 after steering oil, mining and power-sector reforms through the legislature. (Al Jazeera)
  • National Liberation Army (ELN) — Colombian guerrilla group operating along the 2,200km border, whose camp in Apure state was hit by a Venezuelan army attack on 30 October 2026 that killed at least 19 people. (ewn.co.za)
  • Foro Penal — Venezuelan legal rights group that represents detainees and says more than 320 political prisoners remain held despite releases under the 2026 amnesty law. (thestar.com.my)

Timeline

  1. September 2, 2025 — US forces strike a boat that Washington says left Venezuela carrying drugs, killing 11 people in the first strike of the maritime campaign. (dawn.com)
  2. October 2025 — UN human rights chief Volker Türk calls the US boat strikes unacceptable after the 14th strike raises the death toll to at least 61. (PBS NewsHour)
  3. January 3, 2026 — US strikes hit Caracas and special forces capture Maduro and Cilia Flores, who are flown to New York for prosecution. (PBS NewsHour)
  4. January 5, 2026 — Maduro pleads not guilty in a New York court as Delcy Rodríguez is sworn in as interim president in Caracas. (Al Jazeera)
  5. February 19, 2026 — Rodríguez signs an amnesty law; Foro Penal counts 448 releases since 8 January and more than 600 people still held for political reasons. (wlrn.org)
  6. March 30, 2026 — The US formally reopens its embassy in Caracas after restoring full diplomatic relations, ending a seven-year closure. (PBS NewsHour)
  7. June 24, 2026 — Earthquakes of magnitude 7.2 and 7.5 strike northern Venezuela, hitting La Guaira state hardest; the official toll reached 5,278 dead by 20 July. (ewn.co.za)
  8. October 25, 2026 — Opposition leader María Corina Machado returns to Venezuela overland from Colombia after several failed attempts. (dailymaverick.co.za)
  9. October 30, 2026 — A Venezuelan army attack on an ELN camp in Apure state kills at least 19 people, according to FundaRedes and a military source cited by AFP. (ewn.co.za)
  10. November 2, 2026 — Marco Rubio meets Foreign Minister Oliver Blanco in Washington and reaffirms the US three-phase plan for Venezuela. (Al Jazeera)

What to watch

  • Opposition street mobilisation over an election date. The 22 October 2026 protest was the first nationwide opposition protest in nearly two years, and neither Rodríguez nor Trump has set a date for legislative or presidential elections. Tracked in the Protests map layer on the live dashboard.
  • Army operations against armed groups on the Colombian border. An attack on an ELN camp in Apure on 30 October 2026 killed at least 19 people, and a military source told AFP that forces would keep searching for more camps. Tracked in the Armed Conflict Events map layer on the live dashboard.
  • Venezuelan migration and returns. The 2026 regional response plan counts about 6.87 million Venezuelans living in Latin American and Caribbean host countries and estimates that 5.37 million refugees and migrants need assistance. Tracked in the UNHCR Displacement panel on the live dashboard.
  • US sanctions relief tied to oil deals. OFAC removed National Assembly president Jorge Rodríguez from its sanctions list on 14 October 2026; sanctions on Delcy Rodríguez were lifted earlier, and a deal has given the US access to 17 oil fields. Tracked in the Sanctions Pressure panel on the live dashboard.
