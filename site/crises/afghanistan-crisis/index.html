Background

The Taliban reached Kabul on 15 August 2021; UN Secretary-General Antonio Guterres urged 'utmost restraint' and said women's hard-won rights must be protected. On 20 December 2022 the authorities barred women from universities and on 24 December ordered NGOs to stop female staff working, prompting Save the Children, the Norwegian Refugee Council and CARE to suspend programmes. By August 2026 UN agencies counted more than 100 decrees restricting women and an estimated 2.4 million girls excluded from secondary education. Sources: UN News (1, 2), Al Jazeera.

Economic contraction and mass returns, not fighting, drive humanitarian need. IOM counts 22 million people in need, cites a 'persistent macroeconomic contraction' and says over 2.8 million undocumented Afghans returned from Iran and Pakistan in 2025, with returns since 2023 equal to about 11 per cent of the population. Pakistan's 1 November 2023 deadline for undocumented foreigners to leave, affecting an estimated 1.7 million Afghans, opened this era. On 20 October 2026 OCHA's Olga Cherevko said nearly 16 million Afghans face crisis-level hunger, rising to around 19 million in winter. Sources: IOM, VOA, UN News.

Shocks since 2025 compounded the strain. A magnitude 6.0 earthquake on 31 August 2025 in Kunar and Nangarhar killed more than 2,164 people, UN News reported, and days later Taliban security forces barred Afghan female UN staff from UN compounds in Kabul. A nationwide internet shutdown from 29 September 2025 was crippling banking and isolating women, UNAMA said. UNAMA attributes 512 civilian deaths to cross-border violence with Pakistan since October 2025, and in June 2026 the Security Council unanimously urged the Taliban to reverse their crackdown on women. Sources: UN News (1, 2), NY1, WTOP.

Key actors

  • Taliban de facto authorities — Afghanistan's de facto rulers; UNAMA recorded 443 arbitrary arrests by their virtue-and-vice inspectors and a police law banning all demonstrations in July-September 2026. (UN News)
  • UN Assistance Mission in Afghanistan (UNAMA) — UN political mission in Kabul; the Security Council unanimously extended its mandate for one year, until 17 June 2027, under resolution 2822 (2026). (UNAMA)
  • Pakistan — Host to millions of Afghans; it set a 1 November 2023 deadline for undocumented foreigners to leave, its interior minister estimating 1.7 million Afghans affected. (VOA)
  • Iran — Main source of returns in early 2025; the UN reported over 605,000 Afghans returned from Iran in the six months to June 2025. (UN Afghanistan)
  • WFP and FAO — UN food agencies warning of record acute malnutrition; WFP's John Aylieff said in October 2026 'we still have a window to act, but it is closing fast'. (UN News)

Timeline

  1. August 15, 2021 — Taliban forces reached Kabul after capturing provincial capitals over several weeks; Guterres urged all parties to exercise 'utmost restraint'. (UN News)
  2. December 24, 2022 — The Taliban economy ministry ordered NGOs to stop female staff working, days after barring women from universities; several major aid agencies suspended programmes. (Al Jazeera)
  3. November 1, 2023 — Pakistan's deadline for undocumented foreigners to leave took effect; its interior minister estimated 1.7 million Afghans faced forced return, VOA reported. (VOA)
  4. August 31, 2025 — A magnitude 6.0 earthquake struck Kunar and Nangarhar; UN News reported more than 2,164 deaths, at least 3,428 injuries and some 6,700 homes destroyed. (UN News)
  5. September 29, 2025 — A Taliban-ordered internet shutdown became nationwide, the first since 2021; UNAMA said it was crippling banking and isolating women, AP reported. (NY1)
  6. October 19, 2025 — Pakistan and Afghanistan agreed an immediate ceasefire in Doha, mediated by Qatar and Turkiye, after a week of border clashes and Pakistani air strikes. (Al Jazeera)
  7. June 15, 2026 — The Security Council unanimously urged the Taliban to reverse their crackdown on women after at least 30 women were arrested in Herat over dress rules, AP reported. (WTOP)
  8. October 20, 2026 — OCHA spokesperson Olga Cherevko said nearly 16 million Afghans face crisis-level hunger and warned numbers would rise to around 19 million in winter. (UN News)

What to watch

  • Returns from Pakistan and Iran. UNHCR assisted 433,189 returnees between 1 January and 17 October 2026; a surge in weekly arrivals would signal renewed deportation drives in either neighbour. Tracked in the UNHCR Displacement panel on the live dashboard.
  • Nationwide connectivity shutdowns. The September 2025 blackout hit banking, remittances and aviation within hours; a repeat would be an early sign of a new enforcement campaign. Tracked in the Internet Disruptions panel on the live dashboard.
  • Cross-border strikes with Pakistan. UNAMA attributes 512 civilian deaths inside Afghanistan to cross-border violence since October 2025; new strikes in Kunar or Helmand would test the Doha ceasefire. Tracked in the Armed Conflict Events map layer on the live dashboard.
  • Protests over arrests of women. A June 2026 Herat protest over dress-code arrests was dispersed with one person shot dead, UNAMA said; further protests would be a rare public challenge. Tracked in the Protests map layer on the live dashboard.
