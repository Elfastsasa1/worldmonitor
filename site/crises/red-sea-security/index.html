Background

Yemen's war predates the shipping crisis. UN News says government forces backed by a Saudi-led coalition have fought the Houthis (Ansar Allah) since 2014, with the recognised authorities based largely in Aden and the Houthis holding Sana'a and much of the north and west. A UN-brokered truce took effect on 2 April 2022, halting offensive operations inside Yemen and across its borders. OCHA's 2026 plan counts more than 22 million people in need, including 5.2 million internally displaced. Sources: UN News (1, 2), UN Yemen.

The maritime campaign began after the Gaza war. Al Jazeera reports the Houthis launched attacks in October 2023 in support of Palestinians; on 19 November 2023 armed men rappelled from a helicopter onto the car carrier Galaxy Leader near Hodeidah, NBC News reported. After nearly two months of daily US strikes, Oman announced a US-Houthi ceasefire on 6 May 2025 covering the Red Sea and Bab al-Mandab. In July 2025 the Eternity C sank after Houthi attacks that killed at least three crew, AP reported. Sources: Al Jazeera, NBC News, Yahoo News.

Reuters reported in July 2026 that the Houthis declared a naval blockade of Saudi Arabia and fired on Aramco sites in Jizan and Yanbu as the 2022 ceasefire broke down, effectively joining, in Reuters' words, the wider war with Iran. By 10 September UN envoy Hans Grundberg said the Houthis controlled Mokha near Bab al-Mandeb, and Djibouti counted about 3,400 boat arrivals within days, Al Jazeera reported. On 9 October Grundberg said Yemen 'has slid back into full-scale war'; a 10 October strike on Riyadh's airport killed 12 people, Saudi authorities said. Sources: gCaptain, UN Special Envoy for Yemen (1, 2, 3), Al Jazeera.

Key actors

  • Houthis (Ansar Allah) — Known to the UN as Ansar Allah; by October 2026 it was fighting from the Bab al-Mandab coast to Marib and striking Saudi civilian and energy infrastructure. (UN Special Envoy for Yemen)
  • Saudi Arabia — Leads the coalition backing Yemen's government and is bombing the Houthis; Saudi authorities said the 10 October 2026 Riyadh airport strike killed 12 people. (Al Jazeera)
  • Government of Yemen (Presidential Leadership Council) — Aden-based, Saudi-backed government; President Rashad al-Alimi announced in October 2026 'the start of military operations to retake the remaining territory of the republic'. (Newcastle Herald)
  • Djibouti — Receives those fleeing across the strait; authorities counted about 3,400 arrivals at Obock by mid-September 2026 and prepared for up to 10,000. (Al Jazeera)
  • UN Special Envoy Hans Grundberg — UN mediator; on 9 October 2026 he told the Security Council Yemen had 'slid back into full-scale war' and 72 UN colleagues remained detained. (UN Special Envoy for Yemen)

Timeline

  1. April 2, 2022 — A UN-brokered two-month truce took effect; the parties agreed to halt offensive operations inside Yemen and across its borders. (UN News)
  2. May 6, 2025 — Trump said US bombing of Yemen would stop as Oman confirmed a ceasefire under which neither side would target the other in the Red Sea. (Al Jazeera)
  3. July 9, 2025 — The Greek-owned Eternity C sank after hours of Houthi gunfire and drone attacks killed at least three crew, days after the Magic Seas, AP reported. (Yahoo News)
  4. July 25, 2026 — Houthi forces fired on Aramco sites in Jizan and Yanbu after declaring a naval blockade of Saudi Arabia; Reuters said Brent rose above $100. (gCaptain)
  5. August 24, 2026 — The Saudi-operated tanker Amzan was struck 63 nautical miles west of Yanbu, UKMTO said; the Houthis claimed a ballistic missile strike under their Saudi shipping 'ban'. (Al Jazeera)
  6. September 10, 2026 — Grundberg told the Security Council the Houthis had gained control of Mokha near Bab al-Mandeb, ending over four years of relative calm. (UN Special Envoy for Yemen)
  7. October 9, 2026 — Grundberg said Yemen 'has slid back into full-scale war', naming fronts in Taiz, Lahj, the Bab al-Mandab coast, Marib, al-Jawf and Hajjah. (UN Special Envoy for Yemen)
  8. October 10, 2026 — A Houthi strike on Riyadh's King Khalid International Airport killed 12 people and injured over 300, according to Saudi authorities cited by the UN envoy. (UN Special Envoy for Yemen)

What to watch

  • Bab al-Mandab transits. The Security Council warned in September 2026 that Houthi advances in the Bab al-Mandab risk expanding the conflict; falling transits would show that risk being repriced. Tracked in the Chokepoint Status panel on the live dashboard.
  • Strikes on Saudi airports and oil sites. Attacks on Abha and Riyadh airports and Aramco facilities drew the UN envoy's 'grave escalation' warning; new targets would raise the risk to exports via Yanbu. Tracked in the Middle East Strikes map layer on the live dashboard.
  • Boat arrivals in Djibouti. Djibouti logged about 3,400 arrivals at Obock within days in September 2026; renewed crossings would signal fighting returning to Yemen's coast. Tracked in the Displacement Flows map layer on the live dashboard.
  • Taiz and coastal highlands frontline. IOM recorded 252,181 people displaced since the June 2026 escalation as of 18 October; ACLED says the highlands decide whether the Houthis hold their coastal gains. Tracked in the Armed Conflict Events map layer on the live dashboard.
