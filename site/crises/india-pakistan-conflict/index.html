Background

The dispute began with the 1947 Partition of British India, when Kashmir's maharaja acceded to India and the two new states fought their first war over the territory, according to the Council on Foreign Relations. Further wars followed in 1965 and 1971, and the 1972 Simla Agreement drew the Line of Control that still divides Kashmir. India tested a nuclear weapon in 1974 and Pakistan later did the same. In August 2019 India revoked Article 370, ending Jammu and Kashmir's special status. Inside Pakistan, a separate conflict resumed when the Pakistani Taliban (TTP) ended a June 2022 ceasefire that November and announced attacks across the country. Sources: cfr.org, thediplomat.com.

On 22 April 2025 militants killed 26 people near Pahalgam in Indian-administered Kashmir. India blamed Pakistan, which denied involvement. The next day India held the 1960 Indus Waters Treaty in abeyance and closed the Attari-Wagah crossing. In the early hours of 7 May India launched Operation Sindoor against what it called nine terrorist camps in Pakistan; four days of air combat, drone strikes and shelling ended in a ceasefire on 10 May, announced by US President Donald Trump. A Court of Arbitration ruled in June 2025 that India could not suspend the treaty unilaterally. India rejects the court's jurisdiction. Sources: cfr.org, scroll.in, southasianvoices.org, usip.org, dawn.com.

As of early November 2026, the May 2025 ceasefire between India and Pakistan has held, but India still keeps the Indus Waters Treaty suspended. On 15 September an Indian destroyer and a Pakistani patrol vessel collided in the Arabian Sea, and on 2 October Indian border guards shot dead two Pakistani civilians in the Bedian sector, prompting both governments to summon diplomats. Most deaths now come from Pakistan's internal conflict: the PICSS think tank counted 260 deaths from militant attacks in September and 111 in October. Pakistani and Afghan Taliban forces clashed on the border on 19 and 27 October. Sources: cfr.org, pakistantoday.com.pk, arabnews.pk, longwarjournal.org.

Key actors

  • Government of India — Holds the Indus Waters Treaty in abeyance until Pakistan verifiably ends support for cross-border terrorism and rejects the Court of Arbitration's jurisdiction. (tribuneindia.com)
  • Government of Pakistan — Its Foreign Office summoned India's chargé d'affaires on 3 October 2026 over the Bedian killings and demanded a transparent investigation. (pakistantoday.com.pk)
  • Pakistan Army (ISPR) — Runs counterterrorism operations and cross-border strikes, and on 1 October 2026 its spokesman accused the Taliban of hosting militant leaders at a meeting he said India helped arrange. (longwarjournal.org)
  • Tehrik-e-Taliban Pakistan (TTP) — Islamist insurgent group that the 2026 Global Terrorism Index ranked the world's third-deadliest terrorist organisation in 2025; Pakistan says it shelters in Afghanistan. (thediplomat.com)
  • Ittehad ul Mujahideen Pakistan (IMP) — Militant alliance formed in April 2025 with roots in the TTP that claimed the 18 September 2026 suicide attack that killed 31 people. (longwarjournal.org)
  • Balochistan Liberation Army (BLA) — Separatist group that claimed coordinated attacks across Balochistan on 31 January 2026; Pakistan accuses India of backing it, which India denies. (nationalheraldindia.com)
  • Taliban government of Afghanistan — Exchanged fire with Pakistani forces on the Paktika border on 27 October 2026 and said Pakistani drones had struck its security facilities there. (longwarjournal.org)

Timeline

  2. April 22, 2025 — Militants kill 26 people near Pahalgam; the next day India holds the Indus Waters Treaty in abeyance and closes the Attari-Wagah crossing. (scroll.in)
  3. May 7, 2025 — India launches Operation Sindoor against nine sites in Pakistan; four days of fighting end in a ceasefire on 10 May. (southasianvoices.org)
  4. August 8, 2025 — A Court of Arbitration issues its award on the Indus Waters Treaty; India rejects it on 14 August, citing lack of jurisdiction. (tribuneindia.com)
  5. January 31, 2026 — The BLA claims coordinated gun and bomb attacks in Quetta, Gwadar, Mastung and Noshki; Pakistani officials say 145 militants were killed in the response. (nationalheraldindia.com)
  6. February 22, 2026 — Pakistani airstrikes in Nangarhar and Paktika provinces kill at least 13 civilians, according to the UN mission, and border clashes follow. (Al Jazeera)
  7. September 15, 2026 — An Indian destroyer and a Pakistani patrol vessel collide in the Arabian Sea, the first direct military confrontation since May 2025. (cfr.org)
  8. September 18, 2026 — A suicide attack claimed by Ittehad ul Mujahideen Pakistan kills 31 people at the Kohat Police Lines in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa. (thediplomat.com)
  9. October 2, 2026 — Indian border guards kill two Pakistani civilians in the Bedian sector; Pakistan summons India's chargé d'affaires the next day. (pakistantoday.com.pk)
  10. October 27, 2026 — Taliban and Pakistani forces exchange fire on the Paktika–South Waziristan border; Pakistan reports one civilian killed. (longwarjournal.org)

What to watch

  • Incidents on the India–Pakistan border and at sea. The 15 September 2026 naval collision and the 2 October killing of two civilians in the Bedian sector each led both governments to summon diplomats. Tracked in the Escalation Monitor panel on the live dashboard.
  • Militant violence inside Pakistan. PICSS counted militant-attack deaths falling from 260 in September 2026 to 111 in October, while attacks and security operations together killed 272 people in October, 169 of them militants. Tracked in the UCDP Conflict Events panel on the live dashboard.
  • Pakistan–Afghanistan border fighting. Pakistan struck Kunar and Helmand on 30 September and forces clashed on 19 and 27 October; bilateral trade fell 95 percent in fiscal 2026. Tracked in the Armed Conflict Events map layer on the live dashboard.
  • Status of the Indus Waters Treaty. India has held the treaty in abeyance since April 2025 and rejects Court of Arbitration rulings that it cannot suspend it unilaterally. Tracked in the Strategic Risk Overview panel on the live dashboard.
