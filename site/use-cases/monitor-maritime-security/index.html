Monitor maritime security

Monitor maritime security with World Monitor. Compare ship traffic, chokepoints, and conflict reports, then prepare a sourced watch note for your route review.

Open this monitoring view

Who this workflow helps

Maritime analysts and shipping security teams reviewing a sea lane or port approach.

When to start

A reported attack, navigation warning, or unusual traffic pattern near a planned transit.

What you will produce

A route watch note with the affected segment, observed traffic, reported threats, source times, and questions for the vessel operator.

End-to-end workflow

  1. Define the transit segment

    Record the chokepoint, port approaches, transit window, and vessel exposure. Use the trade-route layer to locate the corridor. A drawn route is geographic context, not the planned track of your vessel.

  2. Inspect available ship traffic

    Open the ship-traffic layer and inspect timestamps around the segment. Compare several observations before describing a change. Missing AIS positions can reflect reception gaps or disabled transmitters.

  3. Check the reported threat

    Compare hotspot reporting with the original maritime authority notice and operator statements. Record incident time separately from publication time. Several outlets repeating one notice are one source.

  4. Write the route watch note

    Separate observed traffic, reported incidents, and your interpretation. Assign a review time and the person who will obtain operator confirmation before a transit decision.

Worked example

Illustrative scenario: a report describes an incident near Bab el-Mandeb while ship positions remain visible. Record the incident location and notice time. Visible traffic does not establish that the corridor is safe. Ask the operator whether the planned transit intersects the reported area, and retain the unanswered question in the watch note.

Provenance, freshness, and limits

AIS coverage is incomplete. A static trade route does not prove a vessel itinerary, and an incident report does not establish a port closure. World Monitor supports a watch review; vessel operators and relevant authorities own navigation decisions.

Check the observation time and original source before each decision. This guide describes a repeatable procedure, not a live incident report. Data availability depends on provider coverage and the selected product view.

Common question

Does ship traffic prove a route is safe?

No. AIS shows available vessel positions. It does not establish passage safety, insurance acceptance, or permission to transit. Check authority notices and the vessel operator.
