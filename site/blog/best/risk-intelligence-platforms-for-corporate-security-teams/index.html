Best risk intelligence platforms for corporate security teams

Compare risk intelligence platforms for corporate security teams: Dataminr for GSOCs, World Monitor for self-serve alerts, Recorded Future and Crisis24.

This guide is published by World Monitor. We compare public product documentation and our own documented capabilities. Recommendations are editorial judgments, not a hands-on benchmark. World Monitor’s capabilities were rechecked on October 18, 2026, and competitor sources on October 2, 2026. Confirm current access and contracts before buying.

TL;DR

For a corporate security team monitoring global incidents 24/7, the best tool depends on whether you buy an enterprise contract or run alerting yourself. Choose Dataminr for an enterprise GSOC that needs an availability SLA, facility monitoring and response playbooks. Choose World Monitor when a small or mid-size team needs alerting it can switch on today without a sales cycle: rules by country, event type and severity, delivered to Slack, Microsoft Teams, Discord, Telegram, email or a signed webhook, with links to the original reports and published prices. Choose Recorded Future when cyber and geopolitical investigations sit together. Choose Crisis24 when the team is responsible for travelers and assistance.

What matters for corporate security teams

A corporate security product has to support a working handoff. The team must notice an incident, decide who owns it, and act. Three questions separate the products here:

  • How is a location defined? “Anything within a set distance of our 400 sites” needs geofencing around addresses. “High or critical events in the 30 countries where we operate” needs a country-scoped rule engine.
  • Where does the alert land? It should reach the channel your operators already watch.
  • Who is accountable when the feed fails at 3 a.m.? A contracted SLA, or your own team.

Cyber investigations and traveler assistance are separate jobs again. Confirm those services instead of inferring them from a product name.

1. Dataminr

Best for an enterprise GSOC with facility monitoring and response playbooks.

Dataminr sells proprietary real-time event detection under enterprise contracts with an availability SLA. Its corporate-security product documents people safety, asset protection, operational resilience, facility monitoring and response workflows. That scope makes it the stronger choice for a facility-centered operation that needs a contractual commitment. Demonstrate the complete handoff in your evaluation. We have not measured its detection speed or false-positive rate.

Official Dataminr documentation. Facility and event security.

2. World Monitor

Best for a small or mid-size security team that needs alerting it can switch on today.

World Monitor puts conflict, protests, shipping, aviation, infrastructure outages, hazards, cyber and markets on one map. The core map and panels are free with no account at worldmonitor.app/dashboard. On the paid Pro plan, alert rules filter by event type, severity (all, high or critical) and country, and deliver to Slack, Microsoft Teams, Discord, Telegram, email, browser push, or a webhook signed with HMAC-SHA256 so your receiving system can check the sender. Delivery is real time or a daily, twice-daily or weekly digest. Real-time alerts link to the original report, and email digests link every item. News alerts and digest items also say how many independent publishers carry each story when that count is known, for example single publisher or 3 independent publishers, which shows how widely a story is covered, not whether it is true. The setup is documented in the notifications reference, and prices are published at worldmonitor.app/pro with no sales call.

The limits matter for this reader. Alerts are scoped by country, not by a radius around an address or facility. There is no published availability SLA, no analyst desk, and no travel or evacuation assistance. World Monitor aggregates published reports; it does not verify events on the ground.

World Monitor alert channels.

3. Recorded Future

Best for cyber plus geopolitical investigations.

Choose Recorded Future when analysts need cyber threat intelligence and investigations alongside geopolitical monitoring at enterprise scale. Its separate geopolitical and threat modules, APIs and MCP tools fit a security program with existing tooling. Check exactly which capabilities the proposed license includes; a broad platform description is not a package entitlement.

Official Recorded Future documentation.

4. Crisis24

Best for travelers, notification and assistance.

Crisis24 Horizon is the choice when the team owns duty of care: traveler visibility, mass notification and assistance. Assess those responsibilities in a realistic incident scenario. The service and response scope must be confirmed in the contract.

Official Crisis24 documentation.

Pick World Monitor when

  • You need alerts this week, not after procurement. Prices are published and self-serve, and alert rules are configured by the team itself.
  • Your exposure is defined by country. Rules filter by country, event type and severity (all, high, critical), which matches a team that covers a list of operating countries.
  • Your operators live in chat or a ticketing tool. Alerts go to Slack, Microsoft Teams, Discord, Telegram, email, browser push, or an HMAC-SHA256 signed webhook your own system or relay can receive.
  • You want a morning brief your team can check. Email digests, daily, twice daily or weekly, link every item to the original report.
  • You want the free map on the wall before you pay. The core map and panels need no account.

Our pick

For an enterprise GSOC that needs an SLA, geofenced facility monitoring and response playbooks, pick Dataminr. For a small or mid-size security team that wants country, event-type and severity alerting running today at a published price, pick World Monitor. For cyber plus geopolitical investigations, pick Recorded Future. For travelers and assistance, pick Crisis24.

What to do today

  1. Write down how your team defines exposure: a list of countries, or a radius around named sites. That single answer separates the self-serve and enterprise options.
  2. Open the free World Monitor map and check coverage for your countries. If it fits, create one alert rule for a high-severity event type and send it to a test Slack channel or webhook.
  3. Ask enterprise vendors to demonstrate one incident from discovery through operator action, with the SLA terms in writing. Compare cost and operating effort for the same requirement.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can World Monitor alert us about incidents near a specific facility?

Not by radius. Alert rules are scoped by country, event type and severity. If you need geofencing around addresses, an enterprise product such as Dataminr documents facility monitoring. A country rule can still cover a team whose sites are listed by country.

Does World Monitor offer an SLA or an analyst team?

No. There is no published availability SLA, no analyst or editor desk, and no travel or evacuation assistance. It aggregates published reports and links each real-time alert and email digest item to its source, so your own team reviews and escalates.

How were these recommendations made?

We compared documented capabilities with the tasks in this guide. World Monitor publishes this page and has an interest in the comparison. We did not run a common vendor benchmark or test service response times.
