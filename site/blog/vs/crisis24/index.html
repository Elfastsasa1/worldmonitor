World Monitor vs Crisis24

World Monitor vs Crisis24 for travel and security risk: Crisis24 covers traveler assistance, while World Monitor offers self-serve country alerts and an API.

This guide is published by World Monitor. We compare public product documentation and our own documented capabilities. Recommendations are editorial judgments, not a hands-on benchmark. World Monitor’s capabilities were rechecked on October 16, 2026, and competitor sources on October 2, 2026. Confirm current access and contracts before buying.

TL;DR

For travel and security risk, choose Crisis24 when your organization has a duty of care: it covers traveler visibility, mass notification and assistance. Choose World Monitor when the job is monitoring, not looking after travelers: a free live map, alert rules by country, event type and severity delivered to Slack, Microsoft Teams, Discord, Telegram, email, browser push or a signed webhook, and a REST API, SDKs and MCP, all at published prices with no sales call.

Monitoring or a duty-of-care service?

A travel manager needs to know who is exposed, contact them, and arrange help. That is a service with people behind it. An analyst or a small security team may only need to know when something happens in the countries they cover and pass it to the right channel. The service behind the interface matters as much as the screen when someone needs assistance.

Where Crisis24 is better

Crisis24 Horizon’s documentation describes risk intelligence, travel risk management, people and site visibility, mass notification, and around-the-clock assistance. We recommend it when the requirement includes operational support for people, rather than monitoring alone. See the Crisis24 Horizon page. Ask which assistance, medical support and response regions the contract includes.

Where World Monitor is better

World Monitor is the better fit for monitoring at a published price. The free map needs no account and shows conflict, protests, shipping, aviation, infrastructure outages, hazards, cyber and markets together. On the Pro plan, alert rules filter by event type, severity (all, high or critical) and country and deliver to Slack, Microsoft Teams, Discord, Telegram, email, browser push, or a webhook signed with HMAC-SHA256. Delivery is real time or a daily, twice-daily or weekly digest. Real-time alerts link to the original report, and email digests link every item (notifications reference).

Developers get a REST API on a separate API plan, Python, Go and Ruby SDKs, an npm CLI, and an MCP server with Pro. Each outlet has an editorial tier and a propaganda-risk band: the News panel shows them as badges, the free get_sources MCP tool returns them for any outlet, and the data sources page explains the scheme. Prices are on the plans page.

The limits that matter

World Monitor offers no travel or evacuation assistance, no analyst desk, and no published availability SLA. It does not know where your travelers are, and alerts are scoped by country, not by a radius around a hotel or office. It aggregates published reports and does not verify events on the ground. Neither a map nor this article is a travel authorization.

Crisis24’s assistance, medical support, response regions and service levels depend on the contract. We have not tested its response.

Pick World Monitor when

  • You monitor, and someone else handles travelers. Country alert rules cover the watching without buying an assistance service.
  • Alerts must reach your team’s channels. Slack, Microsoft Teams, Discord, Telegram, email, browser push or an HMAC-signed webhook.
  • You prepare pre-trip briefings. The free map shows conflict, protests, aviation, outages and hazards for a destination.
  • You need a price before a call. World Monitor publishes its prices.
  • You feed your own systems. The REST API, SDKs and MCP server expose the same data.

Our pick

For traveler visibility, notification and contracted assistance, pick Crisis24. For country-level monitoring and alerts that an analyst or a small security team configures and routes itself, pick World Monitor. If you need both, keep Crisis24 for people and use World Monitor for the wider picture.

What to do today

Ask Crisis24 to demonstrate a disruption affecting a named trip, the message to the traveler, and the assistance handoff. Set World Monitor alert rules for the same destination countries and compare what reached your team. Assess those as separate jobs and record who remains responsible for each action.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is World Monitor a full replacement for Crisis24?

No. Crisis24 is the stronger fit for travel risk, duty of care and assistance. World Monitor covers monitoring and alerts by country, not traveler tracking or help on the ground.

Which option costs less?

World Monitor’s map is free, and its paid prices are on the plans page. Crisis24 sells packages by quote. This guide does not invent a competitor price.

Did you measure which tool is faster?

No. This is a documentation-based comparison. Evaluate source timestamps, gaps, and the action your team can take. We do not claim a measured latency or accuracy advantage.
