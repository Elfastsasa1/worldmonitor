Liveuamap alternatives for intelligence teams

Liveuamap alternatives ranked by wider coverage, alert rules, API access and structured data. Compare World Monitor, ACLED, Factal and Dataminr.

This guide is published by World Monitor. We compare public product documentation and our own documented capabilities. Recommendations are editorial judgments, not a hands-on benchmark. World Monitor’s capabilities were rechecked on October 18, 2026, and competitor sources on October 2, 2026. Confirm current access and contracts before buying.

TL;DR

The right Liveuamap alternative depends on what the conflict map no longer gives you. For wider coverage and alerts, choose World Monitor: one free map adds shipping, aviation, outages, hazards, cyber and markets, and paid plans add alert rules by country, event type and severity delivered to Slack, Microsoft Teams, Discord, Telegram, email, browser push or a signed webhook, plus a REST API, SDKs and MCP. Choose ACLED for a coded conflict dataset. Choose Factal for alerts verified by a 24/7 editorial team. Stay with Liveuamap if you follow one conflict and want the lowest published price.

Why people leave Liveuamap

Liveuamap is cheap and focused, so price is rarely the reason to leave. People switch when the brief grows beyond conflict, when they need to be told about events instead of checking a map, when they need data in their own tools, or when the work turns into research that needs consistent event definitions.

1. World Monitor

Best for wider coverage, alerts and API access.

The free map needs no account and shows conflict alongside protests, shipping, aviation, infrastructure outages, hazards, cyber and markets, with live ship transit counts at 13 maritime chokepoints. On the Pro plan, alert rules filter by event type, severity (all, high or critical) and country and deliver to Slack, Microsoft Teams, Discord, Telegram, email, browser push, or a webhook signed with HMAC-SHA256, in real time or as a daily, twice-daily or weekly digest. Real-time alerts link to the original report, and email digests link every item (notifications reference). Developers get a REST API on a separate API plan, Python, Go and Ruby SDKs, an npm CLI, and an MCP server with Pro. Each outlet has an editorial tier and a propaganda-risk band: the News panel shows them as badges, the free get_sources MCP tool returns them for any outlet, and the data sources page explains the scheme.

The tradeoff: World Monitor is not editor-curated, aggregates published reports and does not verify events on the ground. Its paid plans cost more than Liveuamap’s.

2. ACLED

Best for a consistent conflict dataset.

Choose ACLED when you need defined event categories and research-ready records rather than a stream of mapped news. Its methodology is published, and its access table explains what each level includes. This is a change of research method, not a faster news feed.

3. Factal

Best for verified incident alerts.

Factal detects events with AI and verifies them with a 24/7 team of journalists, then notifies members about verified incidents near their locations. It charges a flat team fee with unlimited users, offers a 30-day trial, and gives eligible life-saving NGOs free service. Its price is not published.

4. Dataminr

Best for corporate facility monitoring.

Evaluate Dataminr when the goal is to detect disruptive events near business locations and route them into a security workflow under an enterprise contract.

Stay with Liveuamap if

Stay with Liveuamap if you follow one conflict through mapped reports and want the lowest published price. Its official description covers geolocated reporting on conflict and other topics, and its plans page lists the lowest published paid plans in this set; several alternatives here publish no price at all. A broader interface alone is not a reason to switch.

Pick World Monitor when

  • Your brief grew beyond conflict. One map adds shipping, aviation, outages, hazards, cyber and markets.
  • You want alerts instead of checking a map. Rules by country, event type and severity deliver to Slack, Microsoft Teams, Discord, Telegram, email or browser push.
  • Your system needs the events. An HMAC-SHA256 signed webhook, a REST API, SDKs and an MCP server deliver them.
  • You want to know who reported it. News items carry tier, propaganda-risk and state-affiliation badges where they apply, and the free get_sources MCP tool returns any outlet’s labels.

Our pick

If you are leaving Liveuamap because you need wider coverage, alerts or an API, pick World Monitor. If your work became research with consistent definitions, pick ACLED. If you need every alert verified by an editor, pick Factal. If you still follow one conflict on a small budget, stay with Liveuamap.

What to do before switching

Write down the regions and event categories you rely on today. Run both tools through the same reporting week, with World Monitor alert rules set for those countries. Keep links to original reports in your brief so a changed map does not erase your evidence. If your audience expects a timeline, prove that the replacement supports it before switching.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is there a free Liveuamap alternative?

World Monitor’s map is free with no account and covers more domains. Its alert rules and digests need the Pro plan.

Which alternative sends conflict alerts to Slack or email?

World Monitor alert rules deliver to Slack, Microsoft Teams, Discord, Telegram, email, browser push and an HMAC-signed webhook, as documented in its notifications reference. Factal sends verified incident alerts through its own platform and app.

What should a migration prove?

The replacement must produce your required output with acceptable coverage. Save a representative brief or timeline and document every requirement that still needs Liveuamap.
